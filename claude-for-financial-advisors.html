Why this question keeps coming up

Anthropic has been shipping finance-focused features on a fast clock, close to every ten weeks, and wealth firms keep getting pulled into the update cycle whether they asked for it or not. Here's a sourced version of that timeline.

  • July 15, 2025

    Anthropic launches Claude for Financial Services, with data connections to providers including S&P Global, Morningstar, and FactSet.

  • October 27, 2025

    Anthropic adds a Claude for Excel plugin and new data connectors, including Moody's, aimed at teams that live in spreadsheets.

  • February 24, 2026

    LPL Financial expands its partnership with Anthropic, bringing Claude to LPL's network of more than 30,000 affiliated advisors.

  • May 5, 2026

    Anthropic ships ten ready-to-use agent templates for financial services plus full Microsoft 365 integration, adding still more data partners.

None of these releases close the client-file gap on their own. They put Claude in front of institutions and large broker-dealer networks like LPL's rather than in front of a solo advisor's desktop, which is exactly why the access methods further down this page exist.

What Claude can already do for an advisor

Sign up for Claude and you get a capable general-purpose model with no advisor-specific features and no memory of your book of business. Inside that plain account, it's still useful for a lot of the writing and thinking work that fills an advisor's week.

  • Drafting client emails, letters, and meeting follow-ups from notes you type or paste in.
  • Summarizing a document you upload for that conversation, a fund prospectus or a financial plan.
  • Explaining a tax, estate, or planning concept in language you can hand to a client.
  • Checking the math on a projection or comparing two scenarios you describe.
  • Drafting notes ahead of a client meeting, if you paste the relevant background in yourself.
  • Brainstorming language for compliance-reviewed marketing copy or a client newsletter.

All of it works from what you type or paste into that one conversation. None of it involves Claude reaching out to your computer, your CRM, or your inbox unless you set that up separately.

What Claude can't see, and why most comparisons skip it

Every new Claude conversation starts with nothing. No memory of a client from last week's chat, no access to the CRM record, no read on the plan you built in March. Claude only knows what you put in front of it, unless you've connected an outside source.

Most articles that compare Claude to other AI tools stop at capability, what it can write, summarize, or calculate, and skip the harder question of what it can reach. That's the decision an advisor actually has to make. Not whether Claude is smart enough, but how, and whether, to let it near client records at all.

Claude connectors, in plain English

Claude can read from and act on other pieces of software through something called a connector, a small program that tells Claude how to talk to a particular system, whether that's Google Drive, a CRM, or a program running on your own computer. Most connectors, including the one Briefly runs, are built on the same open standard for how a model talks to outside tools and data. You don't need to understand that standard any more than you need to understand how the postal system routes mail to use your mailbox. What matters is where the other end of the connector lives, on a vendor's server somewhere, or on your own machine.

How to give Claude access to client files securely

There are four common ways advisors connect Claude to something resembling client information, and they aren't equally safe. The list below is ranked from the least contained to the most.

  1. Pasting into the chat

    You copy a client's situation, notes, or numbers directly into the conversation. It's the fastest way to get an answer, and also the method with the least oversight. That text becomes part of the conversation on Anthropic's servers, and depending on your plan, may be used to improve their models. Fine for a hypothetical or an already de-identified question. Risky for anything a client would recognize as their own.

  2. Claude's built-in connectors

    Claude Desktop has a menu of connectors to apps you probably already use, Google Drive and Gmail among them. Anthropic's own infrastructure handles the connection, so your files stay wherever Google or Microsoft already stores them, but Claude, and by extension Anthropic, reads them during the conversation. No extra install, and one more company sitting in the data path.

  3. A vendor's own connector

    Some financial software companies run their own connector into Claude the same way Anthropic does. In practice, this usually means the vendor holds a copy of your client data on their own servers so the connector has something to serve up when Claude asks for it. You're now trusting that company's security and retention practices on top of Anthropic's. Our compliance page goes deeper on the vendor-oversight questions a setup like this raises before you turn one on.

  4. A local connector that reads files from your machine

    The connector runs on your own computer and reads files from a folder you choose. Claude asks it a question, the connector reads the answer off your machine's disk, and Anthropic processes what Claude reads under your firm's own Claude plan, the same model call every method on this list involves. The difference is storage, since this is the only one of the four where client files never end up copied onto a vendor's infrastructure, and it's the approach we built the Briefly connector around. Client files never reach Briefly's servers. Only your sign-in, a check that your seat is active, de-identified preference rules, and a short usage record for each tool call do.

What Claude handles well once it can see your files

The five examples below show up every week once Claude can read a client's files instead of relying on whatever an advisor remembered to type in. Each one assumes a local connector like Briefly's, which reads whatever sits in the folder you point it at and cites the specific file behind every number, rather than a generic answer about what a typical client's situation looks like. Ask Claude for a client's current allocation and it can either guess from general knowledge of how portfolios tend to look, or read the actual statement in front of it and say which document the number came from. Everything below assumes the second version.

Meeting prep from a folder of statements

Drop the last two or three custodian statements for a client into an intake folder and ask for a meeting brief. Claude reads each PDF, pulls the account values, contribution or withdrawal activity, and anything that changed since the last statement, then writes a short brief with every number cited back to the specific statement it came from. The advisor still reads it over before the meeting and fixes anything that looks off. An advisor meeting a client Tuesday can drop Friday's statements in over the weekend and have a brief waiting Monday morning. A brief that used to mean opening the CRM plus three or four PDF tabs turns into a file drop and one question.

Drafting a client email from a plan document

Point Claude at a financial plan already sitting in the folder and ask for a follow-up email explaining one recommendation in plain language, a Roth conversion amount, a new savings rate, a change to the glide path. Because it's reading the actual plan rather than guessing at what a typical plan recommends, the numbers in the draft match the document the client received. The advisor is editing a real first draft instead of staring at a blank compose window, and still runs it through the firm's normal compliance review before sending, the same as any other client communication.

Summarizing a custodian PDF

A forty-page brokerage statement is not something anyone wants to read cover to cover before a call. Ask Claude to summarize holdings, cash flows, and anything that changed since the prior statement. It comes back with a short summary, for example that a client's equity allocation drifted three points higher after a rally and their cash position doubled after a required distribution, and cites the source PDF next to each figure so the advisor can click through and check any number against the original before repeating it to a client.

Checking concentration across accounts

A client holding the same stock in a taxable account, an IRA, and a 401(k) held away at a different custodian usually means opening three statements and adding the position up by hand. Claude can total that position across every statement on file and flag what share of the household's assets sit in one name, whether that's 12 percent or 40, with the source statement cited for each account the number came from, so the advisor isn't taking the total on faith.

Quarterly review notes

Ahead of a quarterly review, ask Claude to pull performance numbers, the notes from the last meeting, and any open follow-ups into one outline. It reads what's already sitting in the folder instead of the advisor reconstructing the quarter from memory or old email threads, so the advisor walks into the review already knowing what changed and why. Every figure in the outline traces back to the statement or note it came from.

The mechanism is the same in every example above. The connector hands Claude the actual file, and every answer points back to the line the number came from, instead of a fluent-sounding paragraph nobody can trace to anything.

Getting started

If you're picking this up for the first time, start simple and add access only as you need it.

  1. Pick a Claude plan that matches how you'll use it. Anthropic's commercial terms bar training on customer content for Work, Enterprise, and API accounts, while personal plans train on conversations unless you opt out. Many firms keep client work on a business plan for that reason, and your compliance officer can weigh in on the right setup for yours.
  2. Start with pasting or a built-in connector for occasional use, methods one or two above.
  3. If you want Claude reading your files regularly, decide between a vendor's own connector and a local connector based on where you're comfortable having that data sit.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use Claude for financial advisor work today?

Yes. Claude works like any general AI chat tool out of the box, drafting emails, summarizing documents you paste in, and explaining planning or tax concepts. It has no special financial advisor mode and no access to your client files unless you connect something yourself.

What can Claude do without any extra setup?

Draft client communication, summarize a document you paste in, check math, explain a concept in plain language, and help write compliance-reviewed marketing copy. All of this works inside a plain Claude account. None of it involves Claude reading anything from your computer or your CRM on its own.

Can Claude see my client files automatically?

No. Claude does not browse your computer, your CRM, or your email on its own. Anything it knows about a client came from what someone gave it, whether pasted into a chat, carried forward by its memory feature from an earlier chat, or read through an approved connector.

What is a Claude connector?

A Claude connector is a small program that lets Claude read and act on a firm's own systems and files, whether that's a calendar, a CRM, or a program running on your own computer. You don't need to understand how it works under the hood. What matters is where the other end of the connector lives, a vendor's server or your own machine.

Is it safe to paste client information directly into Claude?

It depends on your plan. On Claude for Work, Enterprise, and API accounts, Anthropic's commercial terms bar training on customer content. A personal account trains on conversations unless you opt out in settings, and safety-flagged chats can be used even then. There's also a recordkeeping question, since what you paste may count as business correspondence under your firm's own rules, which is worth confirming with your compliance officer.

What's the difference between Claude's built-in connectors and a vendor's own connector?

Claude's built-in connectors, like Google Drive or Gmail, route through Anthropic's own infrastructure to reach an app you already use. A vendor's own connector works differently, and it often means the vendor has copied your data onto their servers so there's something for Claude to read.

Does Claude remember a client from one conversation to the next?

Partly. Claude's memory is on by default now and carries context between conversations, so a client you discussed last week can come up again. That memory lives in one person's account though, where the firm can't review it, correct it, or cite it, and nobody else on the team inherits it. Briefly writes what gets learned about a client back to files your firm owns, so every advisor's next conversation starts from the same record, with citations.

How is Briefly different from just using Claude on its own?

Briefly is an AI knowledge base for financial advisors that runs inside Claude as a local connector. It reads and writes files in a folder on your computer instead of a vendor's cloud, so Claude works from your actual client records and Briefly never holds a copy of them.
