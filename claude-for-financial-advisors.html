Claude for Financial Advisors

What Claude can do for financial advisors, and what it still can't see.

A plain look at what Claude handles out of the box, why your client files aren't part of that by default, and four ways to close the gap, ranked by where your data actually ends up.

Briefly Team Published July 23, 2026 Last verified July 23, 2026

A disclosure before we get into it. Briefly is the local connector we built, so we're not a neutral party on this topic. Everything below is written to hold up whether or not you ever install it, and the comparison further down scores our own product the same way it scores everyone else's.

Why this question keeps coming up

Anthropic has been shipping finance-focused features on a fast clock, something close to every ten weeks, and wealth firms keep getting pulled into the update cycle whether they asked for it or not. Here's a short version of that timeline.

  • July 2025

    Anthropic launches Claude for Financial Services, with data connections to providers including S&P Global, Moody's, Morningstar, and FactSet.

  • Late 2025

    Anthropic extends Claude's spreadsheet and financial-modeling abilities, aimed at teams that live in Excel and portfolio-management tools.

  • Early 2026

    More financial-data and workflow partnerships roll out as part of Anthropic's regular release cadence, each one adding a new data source Claude can reach with permission.

  • 2026

    LPL Financial partners with Anthropic, bringing Claude to LPL's network of more than 30,000 affiliated advisors.

None of these releases close the client-file gap on their own. They make Claude more capable in general, not more able to see your specific clients, which is the part this page is actually about.

What Claude can already do for an advisor

Sign up for Claude and you get a capable general-purpose model with no advisor-specific features and no memory of your book of business. Inside that plain account, it's still useful for a lot of the writing and thinking work that fills an advisor's week.

  • Drafting client emails, letters, and meeting follow-ups from notes you type or paste in.
  • Summarizing a document you upload for that conversation, a fund prospectus or a financial plan.
  • Explaining a tax, estate, or planning concept in language you can hand to a client.
  • Checking the math on a projection or comparing two scenarios you describe.
  • Drafting notes ahead of a client meeting, if you paste the relevant background in yourself.
  • Brainstorming language for compliance-reviewed marketing copy or a client newsletter.

All of it works from what you type or paste into that one conversation. None of it involves Claude reaching out to your computer, your CRM, or your inbox unless you set that up separately.

What Claude can't see, and why most comparisons skip it

Every new Claude conversation starts with nothing. No memory of a client from last week's chat, no access to the CRM record, no read on the plan you built in March. Claude only knows what you put in front of it, unless you've connected an outside source.

Most articles that compare Claude to other AI tools stop at capability, what it can write, summarize, or calculate, and skip the harder question of what it can reach. That's the decision an advisor actually has to make. Not whether Claude is smart enough, but how, and whether, to let it near client records at all.

Connectors and MCP, in plain English

Claude can read from and act on other pieces of software through something called a connector, a small program that tells Claude how to talk to a particular system, whether that's Google Drive, a CRM, or a program running on your own computer. Most connectors, including the one Briefly runs, are built on a standard called MCP (Model Context Protocol), which is Anthropic's open specification for how a model talks to outside tools and data. You don't need to understand the standard any more than you need to understand how the postal system routes mail to use your mailbox. What matters is where the other end of that connector lives, on a vendor's server somewhere, or on your own machine.

How to give Claude access to client files securely

There are four common ways advisors connect Claude to something resembling client information, and they aren't equally safe. The list below is ranked from the least contained to the most.

  1. Pasting into the chat

    You copy a client's situation, notes, or numbers directly into the conversation. It's the fastest way to get an answer, and also the method with the least oversight. That text becomes part of the conversation on Anthropic's servers, and depending on your plan, may be used to improve their models. Fine for a hypothetical or an already de-identified question. Risky for anything a client would recognize as their own.

  2. Claude's built-in connectors

    Claude Desktop has a menu of connectors to apps you probably already use, Google Drive and Gmail among them. Anthropic's own infrastructure handles the connection, so your files stay wherever Google or Microsoft already stores them, but Claude, and by extension Anthropic, reads them during the conversation. No extra install, and one more company sitting in the data path.

  3. A vendor's MCP server

    Some financial software companies run their own connector into Claude the same way Anthropic does. In practice, this usually means the vendor holds a copy of your client data on their own servers so the connector has something to serve up when Claude asks for it. You're now trusting that company's security and retention practices on top of Anthropic's. Our compliance page goes deeper on what regulators expect from a setup like this before you turn one on.

  4. A local connector that reads files from your machine

    The connector runs on your own computer and reads files from a folder you choose. Claude asks it a question, the connector reads the answer off your machine's disk, and nothing about that specific client goes to a server, not the vendor's, not Anthropic's, beyond the ordinary API call Claude already makes to think. This is the approach we built the Briefly connector around, and it's the only one of the four where client files never end up copied onto someone else's infrastructure. With the Briefly connector, client files never leave your computer. Only your sign-in and de-identified preference rules reach Briefly's server.

Getting started

If you're picking this up for the first time, start simple and add access only as you need it.

  1. Pick a Claude plan that matches how you'll use it. Personal plans train on your conversations by default unless you opt out, while Work, Enterprise, and API plans generally don't. Check before you paste anything client-related.
  2. Start with pasting or a built-in connector for occasional use, methods one or two above.
  3. If you want Claude reading your files regularly, decide between a vendor's MCP server and a local connector based on where you're comfortable having that data sit.
  4. If a local connector is what you want, Briefly is one option, an AI knowledge base for financial advisors that runs inside Claude and reads from a folder on your computer. See how it works before deciding anything.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use Claude for financial advisor work today?

Yes. Claude works like any general AI chat tool out of the box, drafting emails, summarizing documents you paste in, and explaining planning or tax concepts. It has no special financial advisor mode and no access to your client files unless you connect something yourself.

What can Claude do without any extra setup?

Draft client communication, summarize a document you paste in, check math, explain a concept in plain language, and help write compliance-reviewed marketing copy. All of this works inside a plain Claude account. None of it involves Claude reading anything from your computer or your CRM on its own.

Can Claude see my client files automatically?

No. A new Claude conversation starts with nothing about your clients unless you paste details in or connect a source yourself. Claude does not browse your computer, your CRM, or your email on its own. Anything it knows about a client came from what you gave it in that conversation, or from an approved connector.

What is MCP?

MCP, short for Model Context Protocol, is the open standard Anthropic built for connecting Claude to outside tools and data, whether that's a calendar, a CRM, or a program running on your own computer. You don't need to understand the standard itself. What matters is where the other end of the connector lives, a vendor's server or your own machine.

Is it safe to paste client information directly into Claude?

It depends on your plan and what you paste. A personal Claude account may use your conversations to improve its models unless you opt out, and pasted text becomes part of Anthropic's records either way. Work, Enterprise, and API plans typically don't train on your data, but recordkeeping and retention rules still apply.

What's the difference between Claude's built-in connectors and a vendor's MCP server?

Claude's built-in connectors, like Google Drive or Gmail, route through Anthropic's own infrastructure to reach an app you already use. A vendor's MCP server is that vendor's own connector instead, and it often means the vendor has copied your data onto their servers so there's something for Claude to read.

Does Claude remember a client from one conversation to the next?

Not on its own. Each new chat starts blank, so you'd need to paste the same background in again or keep a connector active that can pull it back up. Tools built around Claude, including Briefly, can write what gets learned about a client back to files, so the next conversation starts from there.

How is Briefly different from just using Claude on its own?

Briefly is an AI knowledge base for financial advisors that runs inside Claude as a local connector. It reads and writes files in a folder on your computer instead of a vendor's cloud, so Claude can work from your actual client records without those records leaving your computer.
