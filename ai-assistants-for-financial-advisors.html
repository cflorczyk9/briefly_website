One at a time

What each tool does well and who it fits.

Jump

Jump says it is used by 45,000+ advisors across 11,500+ firms, and it names LPL, Osaic and Cetera as customers.1, 2 The notetaker, pre-meeting prep, follow-up and CRM sync sit in the Meet plan, listed at $100 per advisor per month, with Onboard and Grow as $50 add-ons.3 Its integration list is broad, with Redtail embedded, Schwab, Orion and Black Diamond on the custodian side, and 40+ integrations in all.1, 2, 5 It states SOC 2 Type II, and says nothing reaches a client or a system of record until an advisor approves it.4 Jump fits firms that run meeting-heavy workflows and want account opening, CRM sync and notes in the same tool. We did not find a market brief or prospect analysis feature on its site.

Zocks

Zocks shows three tiers and their prices on the site. Annual plans run $67, $117 and $184 per user per month, and every plan includes unlimited meetings.9 It describes itself as a no-recording notetaker. Transcripts and summaries are kept and can be deleted on a schedule you set.10 Its document tool reads brokerage statements, estate plans and intake forms and syncs to eMoney, RightCapital, PreciseFP and Holistiplan, and it sits on the Ultimate tier.9, 11 An advisor can also ask a question across the book and get a client list back.15 Zocks states SOC 2 Type 2 certification from early November 2023.10 It fits advisors and teams that want planning-tool field sync and a stated price.

Zeplyn

Zeplyn calls itself an agentic operating system for wealth management and says it coordinates 50+ specialized agents from one control layer.19 Its product page covers recording-free notes, prep, follow-up drafts, document extraction, and account opening that fills the custodian form for advisor review. It also surfaces signals such as RMDs, idle cash and Roth candidates.17 It states SOC 2 Type II, SSO and MFA, and zero data retention agreements with its AI providers.18 Zeplyn lists no price and asks buyers to book a 30-minute demo.16 It fits firms that want automation beyond the meeting and enterprise options such as single tenancy, and that are fine getting a quote first.19

Briefly

Briefly keeps a current picture of each client from email, CRM, notes and statements, and every line links to its source. It drafts client briefs, agendas, follow-ups, market briefs and prospect analyses, and sends approved updates to Salesforce. The web app is set up for your firm with Salesforce and Outlook connected, and it costs $150 per seat per month. Client data stays in a private environment for your firm on AWS, and model calls run on zero-retention terms. It fits firms on Salesforce and Outlook that want briefs, market briefs and prospect analyses tied back to their own documents.

Briefly

Where Briefly is different.

Every line in a Briefly brief or answer links to the email, statement or transcript it came from. Jump describes a cited briefing, Zocks shows the source behind its prep data, and Zeplyn describes sourced answers.

Briefly also writes market briefs and prospect analyses. The market brief agent does web research with numbered sources, next to outside house views and your firm's own. A prospect analysis takes five intake answers and the statements a prospect shares, runs up to nine checks, and ends with a list of what it could not verify. We did not find either feature on the competitor pages we read.

Firm view turns your CIO's notes into a dated timeline and one shared summary that shows up in the briefs. Scheduled runs on the Agents tab email each finished report with a PDF attached, and your firm's logo and two colors go on every agent output.

Briefly costs more per seat than the published entry plans. The web app is $150 per seat per month, against Jump's $100 Meet plan and the Zocks annual tiers of $67 to $184. The plans cover different things, so compare scope along with price. Briefly's security page lists no SOC 2 report, which Jump, Zocks and Zeplyn each state. Its site also does not state live meeting capture, account opening or form filling. Its integrations are Salesforce and Outlook, with Orion data and alerts rolling out firm by firm.

Choosing

How to choose.

  1. If you want every client meeting captured and written up, start with Jump, Zocks or Zeplyn. Their sites describe it, and Briefly's does not.
  2. If your compliance reviewer needs a SOC 2 report this quarter, the three competitors each state one. Briefly's security page lists none and lays out its hosting, access and model terms for your vendor file.
  3. If you want account opening and form filling in the same tool, look at Jump, Zocks or Zeplyn, which each describe it.
  4. If most of your time goes to briefs, market briefs and prospect analyses on Salesforce and Outlook, with a source on every line, look at Briefly. If you need a public price before a call, Jump and Zocks publish one and Zeplyn sends you to a demo.
