Your Brain Doesn't Have Enough RAM

By Connor Florczyk · January 3, 2026 · 3 min read

You have 200 unread emails. Somewhere in there is a detail your client mentioned three weeks ago that you promised to follow up on. The meeting is in twenty minutes. This isn't a failure of effort. It's a failure of biology.

The Core Problem

Dunbar's number limits us to roughly 150 meaningful relationships. The average financial advisor manages 100 households. A therapist sees 40 clients per week. A teacher has 150 students. Each relationship involves not just names and faces, but context: family dynamics, past conversations, unspoken concerns, and years of accumulated history.

Memory has hard limits. You can hold about four to seven things in your head at once. We forget roughly 70% of new information within a day unless we actively work to remember it. Task work gets completed. Relationship work accumulates indefinitely.

Why Current Tools Fail

CRMs track the data. Email holds the conversations. Meeting notes capture the context. Portfolio systems show the numbers. But none of them talk to each other, and none of them know what you actually need to remember for the meeting in twenty minutes.

The Real Constraint

The solution isn't to remember everything. It's to get the right information in front of you at the right time so your brain can focus on what it actually does well: reading people, understanding what someone values, and helping them navigate hard decisions.

No one can remember everything about 100 people across years of interactions. That's not a personal failure. It's just how humans work. The question is whether we're going to keep pretending otherwise, or build systems that let people focus on what makes them irreplaceable.
